Task 4 - Open Addressing vs Chaining on seq1

4a) Open addressing with m=90 and 100 input values:
inserted = 87
rejected = 13
unused slots after insertion = 3
alpha = 0.9667
max unique probe positions (sampled keys) = 20 of 90
Reason: quadratic probing f can cycle through only a subset of slots for non-prime m,
so insert may fail even when other slots are still free.

4b) Open addressing with m=89:
inserted = 89
rejected = 11
unused slots after insertion = 0
alpha = 1.0000
max unique probe positions (sampled keys) = 45 of 89
Prime table sizes are advantageous because probe sequences are less likely to get trapped
in short cycles, so more slots become reachable.

4c) Chaining with m=20 and seq1:
inserted = 77
alpha = 3.8500
Chaining can store all (distinct) keys by extending chains in occupied slots.

4d) Theoretical mean probe count 1/(1-alpha):
for alpha = 0.5: 2.0000
for alpha = 0.9: 10.0000
Practical note: open addressing is usually still comfortable up to roughly alpha <= 0.7;
near 0.9 probe lengths become large and performance degrades strongly.
